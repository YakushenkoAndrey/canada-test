Canada is the [[second largest country on earth—10 million square kilometres|number]]. [[Three oceans|number]] line Canada’s frontiers: the [[Pacific Ocean in the west, the Atlantic Ocean in the east, and the Arctic Ocean to the north|place]]. Along the southern edge of Canada lies the [[Canada-United States boundary|place]]. Both Canada and the U.S.A. are committed to a safe, secure and efficient frontier.

@block regions-03 section 44
@heading The National Capital
[[Ottawa|place]], located on the Ottawa River, was chosen as the capital in [[1857 by Queen Victoria|date]], the great-great-grandmother of Queen Elizabeth II. Today it is Canada’s [[fourth largest metropolitan area|number]]. The [[National Capital Region, 4,700 square kilometres|number]] surrounding Ottawa, preserves and enhances the area’s built heritage and natural environment.

@block regions-04 section 44
@heading Provinces and Territories
Canada has [[ten provinces and three territories|number]]. Each province and territory has its own capital city. You should know the [[capital of your province or territory as well as that of Canada|right-duty]].

@block regions-07 section 46
@heading The Atlantic provinces
Atlantic Canada’s coasts and natural resources, including [[fishing, farming, forestry and mining|definition]], have made these provinces an important part of Canada’s history and development. The Atlantic Ocean brings [[cool winters and cool humid summers|definition]].

@block regions-08 section 46
@heading Newfoundland and Labrador
@img p46-nl-edge.jpg “The Edge,” Newfoundland and Labrador
@img p46-nl-flag.jpg Flag of Newfoundland and Labrador
@img p46-moose.jpg Moose
Newfoundland and Labrador is the [[most easterly point in North America|place]] and has its [[own time zone|definition]]. In addition to its natural beauty, the province has a unique heritage linked to the sea. The [[oldest colony of the British Empire|definition]] and a strategic prize in Canada’s early history, the province has long been known for its [[fisheries|definition]], coastal fishing villages and distinct culture. Today [[off-shore oil and gas extraction|definition]] contributes a substantial part of the economy. Labrador also has immense [[hydro-electric resources|definition]].

@block regions-09 section 46
@heading Prince Edward Island
@img p46-point-prim.jpg Point Prim, Prince Edward Island
@img p46-pei-flag.jpg Flag of Prince Edward Island
@img p46-anne.jpg Anne of Green Gables, Prince Edward Island
Prince Edward Island (P.E.I.) is the [[smallest province|place]], known for its beaches, red soil and agriculture, especially [[potatoes|definition]]. P.E.I. is the [[birthplace of Confederation|definition]], connected to mainland Canada by one of the longest continuous multispan bridges in the world, the [[Confederation Bridge|symbol]]. [[Anne of Green Gables|symbol]], set in P.E.I. by [[Lucy Maud Montgomery|person]], is a much-loved story about the adventures of a little red-headed orphan girl.

@block regions-10 section 46
@heading Nova Scotia
@img p46-halifax-ships.jpg Destroyer HMCS Athabasca and HMCS Toronto sail through Halifax Harbour, 2009
@img p46-ns-flag.jpg Flag of Nova Scotia
@img p46-cabot-trail.jpg Cabot Trail, Nova Scotia
Nova Scotia is the [[most populous Atlantic Province|place]], with a rich history as the gateway to Canada. Known for the [[world’s highest tides in the Bay of Fundy|place]], the province’s identity is linked to shipbuilding, fisheries and shipping. As Canada’s largest east coast port, deep-water and ice-free, the capital, [[Halifax|place]], has played an important role in Atlantic trade and defence and is home to [[Canada’s largest naval base|definition]]. Nova Scotia has a long history of coal mining, forestry and agriculture. Today there is also off-shore oil and gas exploration. The province’s [[Celtic and Gaelic traditions|definition]] sustain a vibrant culture. Nova Scotia is home to [[over 700 annual festivals|number]], including the spectacular military tattoo in Halifax.

@block regions-11 section 47
@heading New Brunswick
@img p47-hopewell.jpg Hopewell Rocks, Bay of Fundy, New Brunswick
@img p47-nb-flag.jpg Flag of New Brunswick
@img p47-whale.jpg Whale
Situated in the [[Appalachian Range|place]], the province was founded by the [[United Empire Loyalists|person]] and has the second largest river system on North America’s Atlantic coastline, the [[St. John River system|place]]. Forestry, agriculture, fisheries, mining, food processing and tourism are the principal industries. Saint John is the largest city, port and manufacturing centre; [[Moncton is the principal Francophone Acadian centre|place]]; and [[Fredericton, the historic capital|place]]. [[New Brunswick is the only officially bilingual province|definition]], and about [[one-third of the population lives and works in French|number]]. The province’s pioneer Loyalist and French cultural heritage and history come alive in street festivals and traditional music.

@block regions-12 section 47
@heading Central Canada
[[More than half the people in Canada|number]] live in cities and towns near the [[Great Lakes and the St. Lawrence River|place]] in southern Quebec and Ontario, known as Central Canada and the [[industrial and manufacturing heartland|definition]]. Southern Ontario and Quebec have [[cold winters and warm humid summers|definition]]. Together, Ontario and Quebec produce [[more than three-quarters of all Canadian manufactured goods|number]].

@block regions-13 section 47
@heading Quebec
@img p47-perce.jpg Rocher Percé
@img p47-qc-flag.jpg Flag of Quebec
@img p47-petit-champlain.jpg Petit Champlain, Québec City
[[Nearly eight million people|number]] live in Quebec, the vast majority along or near the [[St. Lawrence River|place]]. [[More than three-quarters speak French as their first language|number]]. The resources of the [[Canadian Shield|place]] have helped Quebec to develop important industries, including forestry, energy and mining. Quebec is Canada’s [[main producer of pulp and paper|definition]]. The province’s huge supply of fresh water has made it Canada’s [[largest producer of hydro-electricity|definition]]. Quebecers are leaders in cutting-edge industries such as [[pharmaceuticals and aeronautics|definition]]. Quebec films, music, literary works and food have international stature, especially in La Francophonie, an association of French-speaking nations. [[Montreal, Canada’s second largest city|place]] and the [[second largest mainly French-speaking city in the world after Paris|place]], is famous for its cultural diversity.

@block regions-14 section 48
@heading Ontario
@img p48-muskoka.jpg Muskoka Skeleton Lake, Ontario
@img p48-on-flag.jpg Flag of Ontario
@img p48-toronto.jpg Toronto skyline
At [[more than 12 million|number]], the people of Ontario make up [[more than one-third of Canadians|number]]. The large and culturally diverse population, natural resources and strategic location contribute to a vital economy. [[Toronto is the largest city in Canada and the country’s main financial centre|place]]. Many people work in the service or manufacturing industries, which produce a large percentage of Canada’s exports. The [[Niagara region is known for its vineyards, wines and fruit crops|place]]. Ontario farmers raise dairy and beef cattle, poultry, and vegetable and grain crops. Founded by United Empire Loyalists, Ontario also has the [[largest French-speaking population outside of Quebec|definition]], with a proud history of preserving their language and culture. There are [[five Great Lakes|number]] located between Ontario and the United States: [[Lake Ontario, Lake Erie, Lake Huron, Lake Michigan (in the U.S.A.) and Lake Superior|place]], the [[largest freshwater lake in the world|place]].

@block regions-15 section 48
@heading The Prairie Provinces
[[Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Alberta are the Prairie Provinces|place]], rich in [[energy resources and some of the most fertile farmland in the world|definition]]. The region is mostly dry, with [[cold winters and hot summers|definition]].

@block regions-16 section 48
@heading Manitoba
@img p48-pisew.jpg Pisew Falls, Manitoba
@img p48-mb-flag.jpg Flag of Manitoba
@img p48-golden-boy.jpg Golden Boy statue
Manitoba’s economy is based on [[agriculture, mining and hydro-electric power generation|definition]]. The province’s most populous city is [[Winnipeg|place]], whose Exchange District includes the most famous street intersection in Canada, [[Portage and Main|place]]. Winnipeg’s French Quarter, [[St. Boniface|place]], has Western Canada’s largest Francophone community at 45,000. Manitoba is also an important centre of [[Ukrainian culture|definition]], with 14% reporting Ukrainian origins, and the [[largest Aboriginal population of any province|number]], at over 15%.

@block regions-17 section 48
@heading Saskatchewan
@img p48-wheat.jpg Wheat fields in Saskatchewan
@img p48-sk-flag.jpg Flag of Saskatchewan
@img p48-coronach.jpg Coronach, Saskatchewan
Saskatchewan, once known as the “[[breadbasket of the world|definition]]” and the “[[wheat province|definition]],” has [[40% of the arable land in Canada|number]] and is the country’s [[largest producer of grains and oilseeds|definition]]. It also boasts the world’s richest deposits of [[uranium and potash|definition]], used in fertilizer, and produces oil and natural gas. Regina, the capital, is home to the [[training academy of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police|definition]]. [[Saskatoon, the largest city|place]], is the headquarters of the mining industry and an important educational, research and technology centre.

@block regions-18 section 49
@heading Alberta
@img p49-rancher.jpg Alberta rancher
@img p49-ab-flag.jpg Flag of Alberta
@img p49-pump-jack.jpg Alberta oil pump jack
Alberta is the [[most populous Prairie province|place]]. The province, and the world-famous [[Lake Louise|place]] in the Rocky Mountains, were both named after [[Princess Louise Caroline Alberta, fourth daughter of Queen Victoria|person]]. Alberta has [[five national parks, including Banff National Park, established in 1885|date]]. The rugged [[Badlands|place]] house some of the world’s richest deposits of prehistoric fossils and dinosaur finds. Alberta is the [[largest producer of oil and gas|definition]], and the [[oil sands|definition]] in the north are being developed as a major energy source. Alberta is also renowned for agriculture, especially for the vast [[cattle ranches|definition]] that make Canada one of the world’s major beef producers.

@block regions-19 section 49
@heading The West Coast
British Columbia is known for its majestic mountains and as [[Canada’s Pacific gateway|place]]. The [[Port of Vancouver, Canada’s largest and busiest|place]], handles billions of dollars in goods traded around the world. Warm airstreams from the Pacific Ocean give the B.C. coast a [[temperate climate|definition]].

@block regions-20 section 49
@heading British Columbia
@img p49-vancouver.jpg Vancouver skyline
@img p49-bc-flag.jpg Flag of British Columbia
@img p49-orca.jpg Orca
British Columbia (B.C.), on the Pacific coast, is [[Canada’s westernmost province|place]], with a population of four million. The Port of Vancouver is our gateway to the [[Asia-Pacific|place]]. About [[one-half of all the goods produced in B.C. are forestry products|number]], including lumber, newsprint, and pulp and paper products—the most valuable forestry industry in Canada. B.C. is also known for mining, fishing, and the fruit orchards and wine industry of the [[Okanagan Valley|place]]. B.C. has the [[most extensive park system in Canada, with approximately 600 provincial parks|number]]. The province’s large Asian communities have made [[Chinese and Punjabi the most spoken languages in the cities after English|definition]]. The capital, [[Victoria|place]], is a tourist centre and headquarters of the [[navy’s Pacific fleet|definition]].

@block regions-21 section 50
@heading The Northern Territories
The Northwest Territories, Nunavut and Yukon contain [[one-third of Canada’s land mass|number]] but have a population of [[only 100,000|number]]. There are gold, lead, copper, diamond and zinc mines. Oil and gas deposits are being developed. The North is often referred to as the “[[Land of the Midnight Sun|definition]]” because at the height of summer, daylight can last up to 24 hours. In winter, the sun disappears and darkness sets in for three months. The Northern territories have long cold winters and short cool summers. Much of the North is made up of [[tundra, the vast rocky Arctic plain|definition]]. Because of the cold Arctic climate, there are no trees on the tundra and the soil is [[permanently frozen|definition]]. Some continue to earn a living by hunting, fishing and trapping. [[Inuit art|symbol]] is sold throughout Canada and around the world.

@block regions-22 section 50
@heading Northwest Territories
@img p50-northern-lights.jpg Northern lights, Northwest Territories
@img p50-nwt-flag.jpg Flag of the Northwest Territories
@img p50-polar-bear.jpg Polar bear
The Northwest Territories (N.W.T.) were originally made up in [[1870|date]] from Rupert’s Land and the North-Western Territory. The capital, [[Yellowknife|place]] (population 20,000), is called the “[[diamond capital of North America|definition]].” [[More than half the population is Aboriginal (Dene, Inuit and Métis)|number]]. The [[Mackenzie River|place]], at 4,200 kilometres, is the [[second-longest river system in North America after the Mississippi|place]] and drains an area of 1.8 million square kilometres.

@block regions-23 section 50
@heading Yukon
@img p50-gold.jpg Family searching for gold, Dawson City, Yukon
@img p50-yk-flag.jpg Flag of Yukon
@img p50-takhini.jpg Takhini Hot Springs Road, Yukon
@img p50-mount-logan.jpg Mount Logan, located in the Yukon, is the highest mountain in Canada
@img p50-logan.jpg Sir William Logan, a world-famous geologist, born in Montreal in 1798. Logan founded and directed the Geological Survey of Canada from 1842 to 1869 and is considered one of Canada’s greatest scientists
Thousands of miners came to the Yukon during the [[Gold Rush of the 1890s|date]], as celebrated in the poetry of [[Robert W. Service|person]]. Mining remains a significant part of the economy. The [[White Pass and Yukon Railway|symbol]] opened from Skagway in neighbouring Alaska to the territorial capital, [[Whitehorse|place]], in 1900 and provides a spectacular tourist excursion across precipitous passes and bridges. Yukon holds the record for the [[coldest temperature ever recorded in Canada (-63°C)|number]].

[[Mount Logan|place]], located in the Yukon, is the [[highest mountain in Canada|place]]. It is named in honour of [[Sir William Logan|person]], a world-famous geologist, born in Montreal in 1798 to Scottish immigrant parents. Logan founded and directed the [[Geological Survey of Canada|definition]] from 1842 to 1869 and is considered one of Canada’s greatest scientists.

@block regions-24 section 51
@heading Nunavut
@img p51-pangnirtung.jpg Pangnirtung, Nunavut
@img p51-nu-flag.jpg Flag of Nunavut
@img p51-inukshuk.jpg Inukshuk, Nunavut
Nunavut, meaning “[[our land|definition]]” in Inuktitut, was established in [[1999|date]] from the eastern part of the Northwest Territories, including all of the former District of Keewatin. The capital is [[Iqaluit, formerly Frobisher Bay|place]], named after the English explorer [[Martin Frobisher|person]], who penetrated the uncharted Arctic for Queen Elizabeth I in 1576. The [[19-member Legislative Assembly chooses a premier and ministers by consensus|definition]]. The population is [[about 85% Inuit|number]], and [[Inuktitut is an official language and the first language in schools|definition]].

@block regions-25 aside 51
@heading The Canadian Rangers
@img p51-rangers.jpg The Canadian Rangers
@img p51-inuit-boy.jpg An Inuit boy in Sanikiluaq, Nunavut, uses a pellet gun to hunt for birds
@img p51-caribou.jpg The caribou (reindeer) is popular game for hunters and a symbol of Canada’s North
Canada’s vast North brings security and sovereignty challenges. Dealing with harsh weather conditions in an isolated region, the [[Canadian Rangers, part of the Canadian Forces Reserves (militia)|definition]], play a key role. Drawing on indigenous knowledge and experience, the Rangers travel by snowmobile in the winter and all-terrain vehicles in the summer from Resolute to the Magnetic North Pole, and [[keep the flag flying in Canada’s Arctic|right-duty]].
