@block economy-01 section 42
@heading A trading nation
Canada has always been a [[trading nation|definition]] and commerce remains the engine of economic growth. As Canadians, we could not maintain our standard of living without engaging in trade with other nations.

In [[1988, Canada enacted free trade with the United States|date]]. [[Mexico became a partner in 1994|date]] in the broader [[North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA)|definition]], with over 444 million people and over $1 trillion in merchandise trade in 2008.

Today, Canada has one of the [[ten largest economies in the world|number]] and is part of the [[G8 group of leading industrialized countries|definition]] with the United States, Germany, the United Kingdom, Italy, France, Japan and Russia.

• [[Service industries|definition]] provide thousands of different jobs in areas like transportation, education, health care, construction, banking, communications, retail services, tourism and government. [[More than 75% of working Canadians now have jobs in service industries|number]].

• [[Manufacturing industries|definition]] make products to sell in Canada and around the world. Manufactured products include paper, high technology equipment, aerospace technology, automobiles, machinery, food, clothing and many other goods. Our [[largest international trading partner is the United States|place]].

• [[Natural resources industries|definition]] include [[forestry, fishing, agriculture, mining and energy|definition]]. These industries have played an important part in the country’s history and development. Today, the economy of many areas of the country still depends on developing natural resources, and a large percentage of Canada’s exports are natural resources commodities.

Canada enjoys close relations with the United States and [[each is the other’s largest trading partner|definition]]. [[Over three-quarters of Canadian exports are destined for the U.S.A.|number]] In fact we have the [[biggest bilateral trading relationship in the world|definition]]. Integrated Canada-U.S.A. supply chains compete with the rest of the world. Canada exports billions of dollars worth of energy products, industrial goods, machinery, equipment, automotive, agricultural, fishing and forestry products, and consumer goods every year. Millions of Canadians and Americans cross every year and in safety what is traditionally known as “[[the world’s longest undefended border|place]].”

At Blaine in the State of Washington, the [[Peace Arch|symbol]], inscribed with the words “[[children of a common mother|definition]]” and “brethren dwelling together in unity,” symbolizes our close ties and common interests.
