The Canadian justice system guarantees everyone [[due process|right-duty]] under the law. Our judicial system is founded on the [[presumption of innocence|right-duty]] in criminal matters, meaning [[everyone is innocent until proven guilty|definition]].

Canada’s legal system is based on a heritage that includes the [[rule of law, freedom under the law, democratic principles and due process|definition]]. [[Due process is the principle that the government must respect all the legal rights a person is entitled to under the law|definition]].

Canada is governed by an organized system of laws. These laws are the written rules intended to guide people in our society. They are [[made by elected representatives|definition]]. [[The courts settle disputes and the police enforce the laws|definition]]. [[The law in Canada applies to everyone, including judges, politicians and the police|right-duty]]. Our laws are intended to provide order in society and a peaceful way to settle disputes, and to express the values and beliefs of Canadians.

@block justice-02 section 37
@heading Courts
@img p37-jury.jpg Jury benches
The [[Supreme Court of Canada is our country’s highest court|symbol]]. The [[Federal Court of Canada|definition]] deals with matters concerning the federal government. In most provinces there is an [[appeal court and a trial court|definition]], sometimes called the [[Court of Queen’s Bench or the Supreme Court|definition]]. There are also provincial courts for lesser offences, [[family courts, traffic courts and small claims courts|definition]] for civil cases involving small sums of money.

@block justice-03 section 37
@heading Police
@img p37-constable.jpg Ottawa police constable helping a young boy at the Aboriginal Day Flotilla
The police are there to [[keep people safe and to enforce the law|right-duty]]. You can ask the police for help in all kinds of situations—if there’s been an accident, if someone has stolen something from you, if you are a victim of assault, if you see a crime taking place or if someone you know has gone missing.

There are different types of police in Canada. There are [[provincial police forces in Ontario and Quebec|place]] and municipal police departments in all provinces. The [[Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP)|symbol]] [[enforce federal laws throughout Canada|right-duty]], and serve as the provincial police in [[all provinces and territories except Ontario and Quebec|place]], as well as in some municipalities. Remember, the police are there to help you.

You can also [[question the police about their service or conduct|right-duty]] if you feel you need to. Almost all police forces in Canada have a process by which you can bring your concerns to the police and seek action.

@block justice-04 section 37
@heading Getting legal help
@img p37-prison.jpg Prisons have an essential role in punishing criminals and deterring crime
[[Lawyers|person]] can help you with legal problems and act for you in court. If you cannot pay for a lawyer, in most communities there are [[legal aid services|right-duty]] available [[free of charge or at a low cost|definition]].
